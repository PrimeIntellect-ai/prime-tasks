Yes. CrowdStrike should promptly file a new Form 8-K under Item 8.01 (Other Events), expressly identifying it as correcting the scope disclosed in the Form 8-K dated July 19, 2024. The filing should explain that Windows devices accounted for most affected devices, Google devices accounted for a substantial further share, and no Apple devices were affected.

The incident disclosures describe impacts only on Windows systems. The Google-device finding is material to CrowdStrike’s investors because it substantially expands the scope of an outage already disclosed as harming the company’s business, customer and partner relationships, reputation, results of operations, and financial condition. A reasonable investor would consider that broader impact important. Google’s size is not the basis for this conclusion, and Google devices should not be equated with Linux systems.

Omitting material information necessary to keep an affirmative disclosure from being misleading exposes the company to potential liability under Exchange Act Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5. Omitting information needed to keep a required statement in a filed report from being misleading also fails Rule 12b-20. The new findings therefore support a prompt corrective disclosure. Item 8.01 is optional, so the recommendation is to use that item for the correction; the item itself does not mandate the filing.
