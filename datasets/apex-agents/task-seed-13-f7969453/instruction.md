Treat the facts below as controlling for this question. Evaluate only whether the exports described below satisfy Section 5.6, "Limited Use of Article 49 Derogations," in PRIV-02 V.2, and assume Northstar's other policy requirements are separately satisfied.

During the first 48 minutes of the EU production outage, Northstar's engineering team made one or two bundled exports of EU production event logs containing personal data to the U.S. analytics vendor. Engineering could not diagnose the EU production failure without them, and the platform was down for every EU customer at the time. Standard contractual clauses with that vendor were still in negotiation and could not have been executed inside the outage window, and no adequacy decision covers the recipient. Nothing was configured to stream on an ongoing basis, and no further exports followed. The engineer who authorised the exports wrote up the basis for them the same evening and sent it to Privacy four hours later; Privacy's on-call reviewer signed off seven hours after the exports, once the incident bridge had stood down.

Under Northstar's Section 5.6 policy requirements, can it reasonably treat these one or two exports as consistent with its Article 49 derogation policy? Reply Yes or No with a brief explanation.
