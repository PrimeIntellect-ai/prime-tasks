Yes, this incident would likely result in violations under Colorado privacy law.

The incident breached the following duties mandated by the Colorado Privacy Act (C.R.S. § 6-1-1308):
-  Northstar breached its duty of transparency (§ 6-1-1308(1)) and purpose specification (§ 6-1-1308(2)) due to the data being processed by an unapproved analytics module without appropriate disclosure.
- Prior consent does not extend to the unapproved vendor, whose use of the data was beyond the original purpose and, therefore, Northstar breached its duty of data minimization (§ 6-1-1308(3)). The data collection was not limited in use for the purpose consent was given, breaching Northstar's due to avoid secondary use (§ 6-1-1308(4)).
- Northstar breached its duty of care (§ 6-1-1308(5)) by failing to implement reasonable security measures, which resulted in consumer data being exported outside of Northstar’s controls.
- Northstar may have failed to ensure that the requisite opt-in consent for processing sensitive data was obtained, which is our duty (§ 6-1-1308(7)). The investigation revealed Northstar does not have adequate privacy safeguards in place and Northstar failed to follow its policy regarding third party vendors and consumer consent in at least one instance.
