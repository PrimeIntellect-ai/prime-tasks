You are a Management Analyst consultant engaged by NovaTrans Logistics. The COO has escalated your firm's engagement following a board meeting: NovaTrans is now evaluating a plant consolidation strategy alongside the original efficiency review. Your engagement brief is in "COO_Engagement_Memo.docx," but the board has added a new directive — identify 3–5 candidate plants for closure or consolidation based on a composite scoring model you must design. NovaTrans operates 19 distribution plants, and the board is concerned that some may be structurally unviable. The COO's memo does not define this term — you must operationalize it using the available data.

Your first deliverable is a Plant Consolidation Scoring Model (Excel workbook, .xlsx) with four tabs. The "Composite_Score" tab should rank all 19 plants on consolidation candidacy using a weighted model with documented weighting rationale. The "Consolidation_Scenarios" tab should model volume redistribution for your top closure candidates, accounting for network topology constraints — port access determines which plants can absorb volume from others — and calculating net cost impact. Source the warehouse capacity data ("WhCapacities.csv"), per-unit warehousing cost data ("WhCosts.csv"), and carrier rate schedules ("FreightRates.csv") from the external Supply Chain Logistics Problem Dataset (Brunel University London, Figshare) referenced in the COO's engagement memo (Section 4: Available Data). The "Risk_Matrix" tab should cross-reference complaint data with closure candidates to assess customer attrition risk. The "Data_Reconciliation" tab should independently verify key operational claims from the engagement memo against the shipment data and reconcile plant code coverage across all datasets.

Your second deliverable is a Board Decision Brief (Word document, .docx, max 5 pages) containing a Methodology Statement, Consolidation Recommendation with supporting metrics and redistribution plans for each candidate, an Implementation Risk Assessment grounded in specific operational and customer data, and a Go/No-Go Matrix recommending one of three actions per candidate: Close, Consolidate, or Retain-and-Monitor. The COO's memo and the board's directive reflect different strategic priorities — your deliverables should address where these align and where they conflict.
