Re: Settlement Proposal - Coastal Property Group, LLC et al. v. Town of Millbrook
    Regarding Ordinance 2025-14 (Short-Term Rental Regulation)

Dear Counsel:

On behalf of our client, the Town of Millbrook ("Town"), we write to propose a framework for resolving the disputes arising from Ordinance 2025-14 without the need for protracted litigation. The Town remains confident in the constitutionality of its ordinance but recognizes the value of an efficient resolution for all parties.

BACKGROUND AND THE TOWN'S POSITION

The Town enacted Ordinance 2025-14 following an exhaustive 14-month study that documented significant adverse impacts of unregulated short-term rentals on housing affordability, neighborhood character, and community welfare. The Study Commission's findings — including a 31% increase in median home prices since 2019 and a 4.2% reduction in available housing stock attributable to STR conversions — establish a robust factual basis for the Town's exercise of its police power authority.

We note that the Fifth Circuit's decision in Hignell-Stark v. City of New Orleans, No. 21-30643 (5th Cir. 2022), strongly supports the Town's position. That court held that STR operators had no cognizable property interest in the renewal of their STR licenses sufficient to support a Takings Clause claim. While we acknowledge the Fourth Circuit has not squarely addressed this issue, the reasoning in Hignell-Stark is persuasive and directly applicable.

Regarding the amortization period, the Town notes that 180 days is consistent with — and in fact more generous than — amortization periods upheld in numerous jurisdictions for nonconforming uses. See, e.g., AVR, Inc. v. City of St. Louis Park, 585 N.W.2d 411 (Minn. Ct. App. 1998) (upholding one-year amortization for adult entertainment businesses); City of Los Angeles v. Gage, 274 P.2d 34 (Cal. Ct. App. 1954) (upholding five-year amortization for nonconforming plumbing use).

SETTLEMENT FRAMEWORK

The Town proposes the following terms, the details of which are also reflected in the database records provided to your office:

1. TRANSITIONAL STR PERMITS (R-2 ZONE PROPERTIES ONLY): The Town will create a Transitional STR Permit category for properties that (a) held valid STR registrations under the prior ordinance regime, and (b) are located in R-2 Residential zoning districts. These transitional permits would be valid for a three-year period ending December 1, 2028, with annual review. During the transitional period, the owner-occupancy requirement would be waived. The annual permit fee would be $750. A cap of 120 rental nights per year would apply (compared to the 90-night cap under the ordinance).

2. R-1 ZONE PROPERTIES: The prohibition on STRs in R-1 zones is a core policy decision by the Town Council and is non-negotiable in this settlement context. For the property at 77 Seaside Terrace (R-1), the Town would be willing to expedite consideration of a rezoning application to R-2, though approval cannot be guaranteed and would require a separate public hearing process with an estimated timeline of 6-9 months.

3. FINE WAIVER: The Town will waive all accumulated per-diem fines ($500/day) that have accrued since the November 28, 2025 amortization deadline for properties that continued STR operations, provided a settlement agreement is executed by January 31, 2026. We understand three of your clients' properties continued operating past the deadline.

4. MUTUAL TERMS: Each party bears its own attorneys' fees and costs. Neither party admits liability. Your clients would dismiss any pending or contemplated legal claims against the Town with prejudice.

We believe this proposal represents a reasonable accommodation that balances the Town's legitimate regulatory interests with your clients' economic concerns. The transitional period provides meaningful time for business planning and property disposition decisions.

Please respond by January 5, 2026, so that we may present any counter-proposal to the Town Council at its January 14 session.
