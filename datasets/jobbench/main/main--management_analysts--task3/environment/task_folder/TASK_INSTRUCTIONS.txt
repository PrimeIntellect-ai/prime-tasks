You are a Management Analyst at the City of Greenfield, where the five departments under review total 596 authorized positions (542 filled): Parks & Recreation, Sanitation, Transportation, Health Services, and Fire Department. The City Council will vote on a departmental restructuring proposal, and you must prepare the decision package.

Proposal A ("Consolidate") would merge Health Services and Parks & Recreation into "Community Wellness" and merge Transportation and Sanitation into "Public Works," with Fire remaining standalone — premised on shared fleet, field crews, and geographic route overlap. Proposal B ("Targeted Fix") would retain all five departments but impose cross-training pools, shared overtime budgets for departments exceeding 10% OT-to-regular-hours, and centralized hiring for divisions with vacancy rates above 12%.

Produce three deliverables using the provided data and any relevant NYC municipal benchmarks sourced separately — including the Mayor's Management Report performance indicators and the NYS Comptroller's analysis of post-pandemic city workforce trends.

The first deliverable is a Decision Briefing Memo (.docx, 6–10 pages) that opens with a situation overview cross-referencing staffing health against citizen service outcomes, followed by a comparative benchmark analysis contextualizing Greenfield's performance relative to NYC analogs. The memo must evaluate each proposal's impact on process bottlenecks, overtime cost structure, service delivery risk for underperforming SLAs, and workforce feasibility — then close with a clear recommendation (one proposal or a defined hybrid) backed by quantitative arguments and explicit assumptions.

The second deliverable is a Cost-Benefit Analysis Workbook (.xlsx) with four tabs. "Baseline Metrics" should present a department scorecard covering vacancy rate, OT percentage, average 311 resolution days, worst on-time process percentage, and Greenfield-vs-NYC per-employee budget ratio, with red conditional formatting applied where vacancy exceeds 12%, OT exceeds 10%, or on-time falls below 65%. "Proposal A Model" should project merged-department headcount, combined OT costs, and cycle times with documented assumptions for overlapping divisions and shared-fleet savings. "Proposal B Model" should show cross-training pool sizes, OT budget caps, and centralized hiring targets, identifying which divisions currently trigger each intervention. "Comparison Summary" should compare both proposals side-by-side on five dimensions — annual cost savings, FTE reallocation count, projected SLA improvement, implementation complexity (1–5), and risk rating (1–5) — with a net recommendation score.

The third deliverable is a Council Presentation (.pptx, 8–12 slides) summarizing current operational health, the trade-offs between proposals in table or chart form, NYC benchmark context, and a recommendation with next steps.

All quantitative claims must be internally consistent across all three deliverables and traceable to source data.

The NYC benchmark analysis should incorporate the externally sourced Mayor's Management Report (MMR) agency performance indicators (NYC Open Data dataset rbed-zzin) for DPR, DSNY, DOT, DOHMH, and FDNY — focusing on service-level agreement metrics and response time targets — and the NYS Comptroller's "2024 Update on New York City Staffing Trends" (Report 2-2025), covering post-pandemic vacancy rates, agency-level attrition, and overtime spending growth.
