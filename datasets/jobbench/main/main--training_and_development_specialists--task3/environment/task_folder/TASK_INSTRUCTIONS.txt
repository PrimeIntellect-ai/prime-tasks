You are the Training and Development Specialist at Acme Precision Manufacturing (200+ employees, eight production departments). The EHS Director has flagged two urgent concerns: the Paint Shop's OSHA recordable rate is nearly double any other department, and corporate has capped Q2 2025 training at $38,000 — roughly 15% less than last year. You must deliver an integrated response addressing both the compliance crisis and the budget constraint.

Your working data includes acme_manufacturing_training_records.xlsx (three sheets: Training Completion Log with 308 records across 45 employees and 10 courses, Safety Incident Log with 52 incidents, and Q2 2025 Resource Availability with classroom bookings and instructor schedules), post_training_survey_responses.csv (338 responses with Likert ratings and open feedback), and Safety_Training_Needs_Analysis.xlsx as generic reference template. You will also need to reference two external online sources: OSHA Publication 2254, "Training Requirements in OSHA Standards" (OSHA 2254-09R 2015, available at osha.gov), for mandatory training standards, and the Kirkpatrick four-level evaluation detail guide from BusinessBalls (accessible via the CTDN TTT Module 5 handout hosted at portal.ct.gov), which includes sample Level 1 survey forms and the NC State Tool-1A retrospective pre/post evaluation instrument.

Your first deliverable is a Cross-Source Compliance Gap Matrix in Excel. The first sheet, "Compliance Matrix," should cross-reference training completion records against OSHA requirements and the needs analysis to produce an employee-by-course matrix for all OSHA-required courses. Each cell must reflect the employee's current compliance standing — accounting for completions, failures, overdue items, upcoming expirations, and courses never attempted — along with the last completion date. Highlight the 10 highest-risk employees using a documented composite risk score that accounts for course compliance failures, departmental incident exposure, and training recency for incident-correlated courses. The second sheet, "Department Risk Dashboard," should aggregate to the department level showing compliance percentage, overdue OSHA item counts, training-related incident counts, average survey satisfaction, and a risk-priority ranking with explanatory notes for each of the eight departments.

Your second deliverable is a Budget-Constrained Q2 2025 Training Plan in Excel covering 13 weeks (April 7 – July 4, 2025). In-person sessions cost $650 for room and materials plus $200 per instructor; virtual sessions cost $250 plus $200 per instructor. The schedule must prioritize courses where incident root causes point to training gaps alongside survey performance, resolving any conflicting signals between these data sources with documented reasoning. All compliance remediation for employees with overdue or failed OSHA courses must be completed within the first four weeks. The plan must respect all instructor and facility constraints from the Resource Availability sheet, maintain monthly coverage for every OSHA-required course linked to training-related incidents, and include a running weekly cost tally that stays within the $38,000 cap.

Your third deliverable is an Integrated Evaluation Framework (PDF, 6–10 pages). Begin with a retrospective analysis identifying the three courses where Level 1 satisfaction most diverges from actual safety outcomes, and articulate what these disconnects reveal about current evaluation practices. Then draft a redesigned post-training survey addressing weaknesses evident in the current data, incorporating a retrospective pre/post self-assessment component. For the two highest composite-risk courses from Deliverable 1, design pre/post knowledge assessments (8–10 items each grounded in specific regulatory standards), a 90-day supervisor observation checklist with observable behaviors tied to applicable OSHA subsections, and three KPIs with baselines drawn from current data and targets for end of Q3 2025. Close with a budget justification projecting ROI by comparing the training investment against expected incident-cost reductions using actual baseline metrics.
